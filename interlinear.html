The Interlinear of Record

The office's claim is that the Arabic governs the sense — and a claim that can be shown need not be argued. This page lays the 1786 sealed grant and its 1836 recordation open word by word: the Arabic of record with transliteration, gloss, and its facsimile-read confidence flag; the carried English quoted exactly as carried; and, on the doctrine-bearing words, summary cards of the Mālikī rules, the period authorities, and the maxims — every summary compressed, labeled, and pinned to its locus in the volumes of record, so that the page educates and the volumes complete.

The model. The interlinear apparatus follows the BibleHub model, enhanced and perfected for a legal-diplomatic instrument (the format of record, Article 21 prototype): where the reference model gives a Strong's number and a modern lexicon, this record gives period anchors — Khalīl's Mukhtaṣar, Johnson (1755), Blackstone, and the locked Volume D glosses — never an anachronistic lexicon; and every Arabic unit carries its confidence flag: [W] word-level verified on the certified facsimile · [S] phrase/strip level · [O] suspended (region confirmed positionally; word-form unresolved — it asserts no reading). The witnesses: A the 1786 sealed book · B the 1786 treaty-form face · 1836 the recordation's Arabic · E the 1786 carried English · E36 the 1836 English · L the Leiden literal.
The discipline. The carried English is quoted as carried — misrenderings are flagged, never silently fixed. Where the facsimile and a carried pin diverge, both are disclosed (see Article 25). Articles not yet transcribed are marked in course of transcription — nothing is printed ahead of the record, and our uncertainty is published, not hidden: a record that shows its flags cannot be accused of concealing them. Every summary ends at its locus: the full rule with its evidences lives in the volumes (the library of record) and is examined in the NI-Ijāzah course.

The preamble

The preamble — the Emperor's declaration of the book

“In the name of Almighty God, This is a Treaty of Peace and Friendship established between us and the United States of America, which is confirmed, and which we have ordered to be written in this Book and sealed with our Royal Seal at our Court of Morocco on the twenty fifth day of the blessed Month of Shaban, in the Year One thousand two hundred, trusting in God it will remain permanent.” — The 1786 preamble of record; the 1836 recordation’s own preamble is noted in the Arabic panel below.
بسم الله الرحمن الرحيم — الحمد لله
The 1786 sealed book opens with the basmala and the seal page (Miller, vol. 2; the book sealed at the court, 25 Shaʿbān 1200). The 1836 recordation opens: “In the name of God, the merciful and Clement! (Abd Errahman Ibenu Kesham whom God exalt!) Praise be to God! This is the copy of the Treaty of peace which we have made with the Americans; and written in this book; affixing thereto our blessed Seal, that, with the help of God, it may remain firm for ever. Written at Meccanez, the City of Olives, on the 3d day of the month Jumad el lahhar, in the year of the Hegira 1252 (corresponding to Sept. 16, A.D. 1836).” (Miller, vol. 4, print 60.)
Verify: the 1836 reproduction, Miller vol. 4, print 34 (physical page = print + 27) · the 1786 sealed-book opening, DOR-AF (PDF page = print − 183).

Article 3 of 25

Prizes — subjects and goods pass free

“If either of the Parties shall be at War with any Nation whatever and take a Prize belonging to that Nation, and there shall be found on board Subjects or Effects belonging to either of the Parties, the Subjects shall be set at Liberty and the Effects returned to the Owners. And if any Goods belonging to any Nation, with whom either of the Parties shall be at War, shall be loaded on Vessels belonging to the other Party, they shall pass free and unmolested without any attempt being made to take or detain them.” — The 1786 carried English of record (8 Stat. 100 tradition; translator of record Issac Cardoza Nuñez, per Barclay’s certificate).

Article 4 of 25

The ʿalāma — the signal by which they are known

“A Signal or Pass shall be given to all Vessels belonging to both Parties, by which they are to be known when they meet at Sea, and if the Commander of a Ship of War of either Party shall have other Ships under his Convoy, the Declaration of the Commander shall alone be sufficient to exempt any of them from examination.” — The 1786 carried English of record (8 Stat. 100 tradition; translator of record Issac Cardoza Nuñez, per Barclay’s certificate).

Article 5 of 25

Examination at sea — two or three men only

“If either of the Parties shall be at War, and shall meet a Vessel at Sea, belonging to the other, it is agreed that if an examination is to be made, it shall be done by sending a Boat with two or three Men only, and if any Gun shall be Bred and injury done without Reason, the offending Party shall make good all damages.” — The 1786 carried English of record (8 Stat. 100 tradition; translator of record Issac Cardoza Nuñez, per Barclay’s certificate).

Article 6 of 25

Under His Majesty's protection — the amān

“If any Moor shall bring Citizens of the United States or their Effects to His Majesty, the Citizens shall immediately be set at Liberty and the Effects restored, and in like Manner, if any Moor not a Subject of these Dominions shall make Prize of any of the Citizens of America or their Effects and bring them into any of the Ports of His Majesty, they shall be immediately released, as they will then be considered as under His Majesty's Protection.” — The 1786 carried English of record (8 Stat. 100 tradition; translator of record Issac Cardoza Nuñez, per Barclay’s certificate).

Article 9 of 25

Cast on shore — particularly under Our protection

“If any Vessel of the United States shall be cast on Shore on any Part of our Coasts, she shall remain at the disposition of the Owners and no one shall attempt going near her without their Approbation, as she is then considered particularly under our Protection; and if any Vessel of the United States shall be forced to put into our Ports, by Stress of weather or otherwise, she shall not be compelled to land her Cargo, but shall remain in tranquillity untill the Commander shall think proper to proceed on his Voyage.” — The 1786 carried English of record (8 Stat. 100 tradition; translator of record Issac Cardoza Nuñez, per Barclay’s certificate).

Article 10 of 25

Engagement within gunshot of the forts

“If any Vessel of either of the Parties shall have an engagement with a Vessel belonging to any of the Christian Powers within gunshot of the Forts of the other, the Vessel so engaged shall be defended and protected as much as possible untill she is in safety; And if any American Vessel shall be cast on shore on the Coast of Wadnoon or any coast thereabout, the People belonging to her shall be protected, and assisted untill by the help of God, they shall be sent to their Country.” — The 1786 carried English of record (8 Stat. 100 tradition; translator of record Issac Cardoza Nuñez, per Barclay’s certificate).

Twenty-four hours before pursuit

Article 15 of 25

Merchants' interpreters — customary rates, not more and not less

“Merchants of both Countries shall employ only such interpreters, & such other Persons to assist them in their Business, as they shall think proper. No Commander of a Vessel shall transport his Cargo on board another Vessel, he shall not be detained in Port, longer than he may think proper, and all persons employed in loading or unloading Goods or in any other Labor whatever, shall be paid at the Customary rates, not more and not less.” — The 1786 carried English of record (8 Stat. 100 tradition; translator of record Issac Cardoza Nuñez, per Barclay’s certificate).

Article 16 of 25

Prisoners exchanged, never enslaved

“In case of a War between the Parties, the Prisoners are not to be made Slaves, but to be exchanged one for another, Captain for Captain, Officer for Officer and one private Man for another; and if there shall prove a deficiency on either side, it shall be made up by the payment of one hundred Mexican Dollars for each Person wanting; And it is agreed that all Prisoners shall be exchanged in twelve Months from the Time of their being taken, and that this exchange may be effected by a Merchant or any other Person authorized by either of the Parties.” — The 1786 carried English of record (8 Stat. 100 tradition; translator of record Issac Cardoza Nuñez, per Barclay’s certificate).

Contrast the word this treaty never uses for the guest: no ownership vocabulary attaches to أَهْل أَمْرِيكَا. **SUMMARY — the full rule with its evidences: the volumes of record.**

The office-function cluster

The Consular Corridor — Articles 20 → 21 → 22

Three consecutive articles seat the treaty’s entire venue architecture in the office, and they read as one corridor. Article 20 leads: the consul decides the guests’ internal disputes — and the assistance clause makes the machinery move, aid “immediately granted” (بِلَا إِمْهَال, without delay): the honorary debt of record, the clause that moves (Companion-J). Article 21 carries the mixed wrong to judgment بِالشَّرْعِ — by the Sacred Law, without increase or decrease, in the presence of the office: the tribunal’s own charter, this site’s namesake. Article 22 closes the corridor: the office — or whoever stands in its place — holds the decedent’s estate in trust until the rightful claimant appears.

The corridor is judicially confirmed: the 1952 judgment, while ending the borrowed capitulatory layer, expressly spared “the special rights under Articles 20 and 21” — the corridor’s first two spans. Walk the corridor word by word below — Article 20 at print 206, Article 21 at print 207, Article 22 at print 208, one folio-page each, in sequence, as the scribe wrote them — then see it operate as the tribunal on the Chapter 21 page.

Article 24 of 25

**The war bar, in the Arabic’s own architecture:** the peace and harmony remain **على حالهما وكمالهما** — in the fullest force — **إلى أن يُطلب … بالصلح** — until the matter is sought by way of peace; judgment between them is **بِإِنْصَاف** (with equity); and only **إلا أن لا يجيب إلى الصلح** — on refusal to answer the peace — does **فلا مانع من وقوع الحرب بينهما** operate. The carried English’s “no appeal shall be made to Arms” has no counterpart words in the Arabic: the war-trigger is the *refusal of the peace-answer*, not anyone’s resort to arms — and even then, **تسعة أشهر** of safe withdrawal. Disagreement is a duty to negotiate, **not** a power to end the grant; the clause carries no notice-and-termination power. **SUMMARY — the full rule with its evidences: ḤIBAH ch. 19 · CORPUS line 67 · the debt-of-honor research note of record (2026-09-11) · studied in Module II.**
