PART I ARCHITECTURAL CORE

THE GOVERNING PRINCIPLE

THE CANONICAL STATEMENT The following principle governs all interpretation of Arti-cle 24 and controls all analysis in this volume:

“When entitlement was granted by the treaty— that is, at the moment the treaty entered into force—the entitlement became part of peace itself. From that point forward, it was removed from discretion, policy, or later reclassification.

Accordingly, no court, administrator, legisla-tive act, or policy instrument could alter it; any attempt to do so constitutes non-performance, a disturbance of the peace, and breach. Histor-ically, such post-entitlement reclassification op-erated through mechanisms analogous to bills of attainder and bills of pains and penalties or sufferings, by which status was altered with-out judicial trial. Article 24 stands as a struc-tural prohibition against those practices by fix-ing entitlement within peace itself, beyond sub-sequent legislative, administrative, or ecclesi-astical manipulation.”

This statement is canonical. It cannot be paraphrased, softened, or reframed. It establishes that treaty-granted en-titlements are constitutive of peace—not benefits flowing from peace, not permissions contingent on administrative grace, but elements that define what peace is. The explicit reference to bills of attainder and bills of pains and penal-ties or sufferings identifies the historical mechanisms by which status-based reclassification was effected without ju-dicial process. Article 24 blocks these mechanisms by archi-tectural design.

WHAT THIS MEANS IN PLAIN TERMS The treaty created peace between two nations. But peace is not merely the absence of fighting. Peace, under this treaty, includes specific protections for specific persons.
