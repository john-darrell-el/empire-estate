Preface

This volume presents the Period Dictionary for The Architecture of Peace treaty commentary series. It contains thirty-two canonical definitions governing interpre-tation of the 1836 Treaty of Peace and Friendship between the Empire of Morocco and the United States of America.

Purpose

The Period Dictionary serves as the authoritative lexicon for all volumes in the Ar-chitecture series. Each term is anchored exclusively in period authorities predating or contemporaneous with the 1836 treaty: Blackstone’s Commentaries on the Laws of England (1765–1769), Vattel’s Law of Nations (Chitty trans., 1834), Kent’s Com-mentaries on American Law (1826–1830), and Wheaton’s Elements of International Law (1836).

Governing Principles

The following principles govern all entries:

1. No Modernization — No term may be interpreted through post-1836 legal

doctrine, international conventions, or contemporary usage. 2. No Proceduralization — This dictionary classifies legal meanings; it does

not instruct enforcement or procedure. 3. No Enforcement Mechanics — The dictionary describes what exists under

the treaty; it does not prescribe remedies or steps. 4. Treaty-Faithful — Each term reflects the juridical meaning operative at the

treaty’s formation. 5. Immutability — Once frozen, entries may not be modified, summarized, re-

organized, or reinterpreted.

CHAPTER 1 Cluster I: Entitlement (Article 24)

This cluster contains five terms governing the Article 24 entitlement structure: GRANTED, ENTITLED, PRIVILEGES, LIBERTIES, and INDULGENCES.

1.0.1

GRANTED 1) Term (verbatim treaty spelling)

Granted 2) Treaty location(s)

Article 24 — “… entitled to the same privileges, liberties, and indulgences which

have been granted to the most favored nation…”

3) Period function Public-law / sovereign-act term (constitutive; not discretionary)

4) Tier-1 — Controlling meaning (authoritative ≤1836)

In period public law, “granted” denotes a completed juridical act of sovereign will that vests immediately upon issuance. A grant is not a promise, favor, or policy choice; it is an act that transfers right or status from the sovereign to the beneficiary and cannot be altered by subordinate authorities once perfected.

• Blackstone treats a grant as a perfected act whereby the sovereign’s will passes

a right that stands unless lawfully resumed by the same sovereign authority

that issued it. • Vattel explains that when a sovereign grants a right by treaty, it becomes bind-

ing and vested upon entry into force; later domestic measures cannot derogate

from the grant without breach.

Architecture of Peace — Volume D • Kent aﬀirms that treaty grants, once in force, are obligatory and not subject

to alteration by municipal acts. • Wheaton confirms that treaty stipulations operate as present engagements

upon ratification and bind the parties as law between them.

(Chicago Notes & Bibliography: Blackstone, Commentaries; Vattel, Law of Na-tions; Kent, Commentaries on American Law; Wheaton, Elements of International Law.)

5) Consolidated 1836 meaning (locked)

GRANTED — A completed sovereign act that transfers rights immediately upon issuance; what is granted vests at once and cannot be revoked or conditioned by subordinate authority without breach.

6) Structural role in treaty architecture • Constitutive Function: Creates entitlements as part of peace itself. • Vesting Moment: Rights vest upon treaty entry into force, not upon later

recognition. • Non-Revocability: Subordinate authorities cannot modify or condition grants. • Article 24 Nexus: What is granted becomes removed from later reclassifica-

tion.

ENTITLED (ENTITLEMENT)

1) Term (verbatim treaty spelling)

Entitled 2) Treaty location(s)

Article 24 — “The United States shall be entitled to the same privileges, liberties,

and indulgences…”

3) Period function Public-law / status term (vested legal right; not discretionary benefit)

4) Tier-1 — Controlling meaning (authoritative ≤1836)

In period public law, “entitled” describes a juridical status whereby the beneficiary holds a vested legal right enforceable as a matter of law, not as a favor or discre-tionary benefit. To be entitled is to possess a legal claim that must be honored by the obligated party.

• Blackstone defines entitlement as the possession of a legal right that cannot

be withheld without injury; it is a status, not a request. • Vattel confirms that treaty entitlements vest upon the treaty’s entry into force

and bind the parties to performance without further condition. • Kent aﬀirms that entitlements under treaty law operate as vested rights that

municipal authorities cannot deny or condition. • Wheaton explains that entitlement under international agreement creates obli-

gations that the parties must perform in good faith.

(Chicago Notes & Bibliography: Blackstone, Commentaries; Vattel, Law of Na-tions; Kent, Commentaries on American Law; Wheaton, Elements of International Law.)
